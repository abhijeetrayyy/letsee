# Ideas for letsee, October 2026

What could make letsee easier to understand, more worth sharing, more alive between people, and a place people come back to — without spending the things the product has already decided to protect. Written 12 October 2026, after the logging, people and series-position work of 5–11 October.

Every idea here keeps to the rules the research set (`WHY_PEOPLE_COME_BACK.md` §6 and §10, `design/PHILOSOPHY.md`): **no streaks, no leaderboards, no badges, no algorithmic feed, no app-started rituals, no taste percentage, no paywall on anything that was free, and nothing that costs a server round trip per view.** Where an idea is "gamification", it is the kind the evidence says keeps the benefit: bounded sets you choose, kudos without rank, similarity instead of score, presence without a breakable chain.

Sizes: **S** a day or two · **M** a week · **L** more than a week. Cost means server cost: everything below reads in the browser under RLS or through routes that already exist, and none adds a cron.

---

## The short version

If I could only do five, in this order:

1. **Waiting for you** — one sentence at the top of Home with the things only you can resolve, by name. The honest reason to open the app. (S)
2. **The stub as a picture** — any diary entry becomes a shareable image, drawn in the browser. Every one shared is an advert for having a profile. (M)
3. **Pass by link** — pass a film to someone who isn't here yet; it waits for them, with your name on it, when they join. The growth loop inside one friend group. (M)
4. **Sets** — "every Nolan", "Best Picture since 2000", a friend's four: bounded, chosen, with progress as pace and overlap with a friend doing the same. The gamification that doesn't corrode. (M–L)
5. **Episode reactions that unlock as you catch up** — the thing TV Time had and nobody rebuilt. (M)

And the cheapest thing of all, before any of them: **one vocabulary** — five verbs, used the same way on every screen.

## 1. Understand it in a minute

The product is simple — mark, log, pass, decide, remember — but it doesn't say so anywhere, and a new person meets its words (room, pass, four, stub, Tonight) one at a time, with no map.

### 1.1 Five verbs, one vocabulary · S · **built 13 Oct**
Pick the five words and use them everywhere: **Mark** (watched · watching · watch later · favourite), **Log** (a day, who was there), **Pass** (give a title to one person), **Decide** (Tonight: pick together), **Remember** (diary, month, year, on this day). Audit every button, heading and toast against the five. Where a screen says "save", "share", "send", "recommend" for the same act, it says one thing. This costs nothing and does more for understanding than any tour.

### 1.2 "How letsee works": five cards · S · **built 12 Oct**
One static page, linked from the welcome, the account menu and every empty state. Five cards, one per verb: a sentence, a real example in your own voice, and the one tap that does it ("Pass a film to someone — try it on this one"). No video, no tour overlay, no step counter. People read five cards; they skip a tour.

### 1.3 The first room, on welcome · M · **built 13 Oct** (in the existing people step, not a new one)
The welcome asks for favourites. Ask the thesis question first: **"Who do you watch with?"** — one name (on letsee or not) and one film you saw together. That makes a first stub, a first room, and the first thing to pass. The product's whole idea in one action, before the person has seen a single list. Favourites come second.

### 1.4 Empty states that do the thing · S · **built 13 Oct**
Every empty page shows one example and a tap that performs it, rather than describing it. Up next, empty: a poster with "Pass this to yourself — it'll wait here". A room, empty: "Pass them something you loved". The example is real (a title from their marks, or a well-known one), so the first tap is a real tap.

## 2. Gamification that keeps the benefit

The research is clear: what corrodes is losing an identity asset. So nothing here can be lost, and nothing ranks one person against another.

### 2.1 Sets · M–L · **built 12 Oct** (keep a list or a person's work; progress as pace; your people on it too)
A **set** is a bounded list someone makes or picks: every film by a director (the person-work route already lists them), a franchise, Best Picture winners by year, a festival's line-up, Ghibli, or simply **a friend's four**. Progress reads as pace, not deficit: "7 of 12 · 3 this year", with a quiet line on completion ("Every one of them") and nothing lost when it lapses. Two people on the same set see each other's progress as overlap: "you've both seen 9; Sam's two you haven't: …". The director rail on a title page already counts "You've seen N of M films" — this generalises it into a thing people choose, keep and share. A set page is a list page with a progress line; membership is counted in the browser from the person's own marks.

### 2.2 A year of nights · S–M · **built 13 Oct** (the calendar was there; nights with someone now lit and named)
A 365-dot calendar on the profile's year page: a dot for each day with a viewing, a face on it when someone was there. Presence without a chain — there is no streak to break and nothing is ever said about a gap. The research's replacement for streaks, and it doubles as the share image for the year.

### 2.3 Kudos on a stub · M · **built 13 Oct** (Want this and Say something, on Activity)
One tap on anyone's diary line or Activity row, and the tap *does* something rather than counts: **"I was there"** (asks to be added to the viewing — the co-log that exists), **"Want this"** (saves it with "from Priya"), **"Told you"** (a one-line reply in the room). Kudos without ranking: the person hears it from you by name, once, and nothing is tallied on a profile.

### 2.4 Overlap moments · S · **already there** (a room's “where you meet”: two you both loved, one you split on)
In a room's header, one line computed from both diaries: "You and Sam gave the same stars to 3 films this year", "12 watched together", "the one you disagreed on: Tenet". Never a percentage. A conversation starter that changes as you watch.

## 3. Sharing

### 3.1 The stub as a picture · M · **built 12 Oct**
Any diary entry — poster, day, where, who, stars, one line — rendered as an image sized for a story or a WhatsApp message, drawn in the browser with the four's colours and a small "on letsee · @ray" in the corner. No server work. Letterboxd's growth came from screenshots of its grids; the stub is the unit of letsee's identity and it is better than a grid because it has a person on it.

### 3.2 Pass by link · M · **already there** (migration 108: pass, night and list links; the invited door; the pass claimed on sign-up)
"Pass to…" gains **anyone**: a link that opens a page with the film, your note and your face. If they join, the pass is already waiting in their Up next with your name on it, and you hear when they watch it. This is the invite the research asked for ("a link to the shared list, sent by text"), but one film is a smaller, truer unit than a list, and it lands the new person inside a room from the first minute.

### 3.3 Our month · S–M · **built 13 Oct**
The month card exists for one person. A room gets one for two: what you watched together, what one passed the other, the one you disagreed on. Shareable the same way.

### 3.4 Lists you can mark as you go · S · **built 12 Oct** (the marks were there; Save all is new)
A list page gets the quick mark on every poster (profiles have it now) and one "Save all to Up next". Someone sent a list of twelve: twelve taps, or one.

## 4. Communication

### 4.1 Episode reactions that unlock as you catch up · M · **built 12 Oct**
In a room, a one-line reaction attached to an episode. The other person sees "2 reactions from Priya waiting at S02 · E04" and they open as that episode is ticked — the spoiler gate decided by their own progress (principle 8). TV Time's founder called this "the ritual"; 25,000 people signed a petition when it went. Nobody has rebuilt it well. The gate is client-side from the watched-episodes cache; the message carries a (show, season, episode) scope and nothing else changes.

### 4.2 After the credits · S · **built 12 Oct**
When two people in a room have both logged the same title within a week, the room offers one prompt when it's opened: "You both watched Parasite — one line each?" Not a push; a thing you find when you come in. The most natural conversation on the platform, made one tap easier.

### 4.3 Asks that close · S · **fixed 12 Oct** (it closed only on a diary entry; marking watched now closes it too — migration 123)
Asks exist ("don't know what to watch? ask your people"). Close the loop the way passes do: when the asker logs one of the answers, the person who suggested it hears "they watched the one you suggested". Silence is designed out (principle 6) — and the answerer is far more likely to answer next time.

### 4.4 Household rooms · M · **built 12 Oct**
Mark a room **"we live together"**. From then on, logging a title from either side offers "both of us" by default and the other's confirmation is one tap in the room; a series shows both positions ("you're at E6, Priya's at E4 — E5 together?"). This is gap #1 in the research — *every tracker records what; none records who* — and it is one flag on a room plus the co-log that already exists.

## 5. Coming back, without being summoned

The rule: the app never creates the moment; it makes the moment after watching easy, and it tells you what is waiting for *you*.

### 5.1 Waiting for you · S–M · **built 12 Oct**
One sentence at the top of Home, finite, by name: "Priya says she was there for Dune · Sam passed you Aftersun · an ask from jojo". Only things that only you can resolve: a co-log to confirm, a pass to answer, an ask, a reply. When there's nothing: "Nothing waiting." No red dot, no count — a sentence with names, which is the one notification style the research found doesn't corrode.

### 5.2 Returns on · S · **built 13 Oct** (on hold rows in Up next; caught-up shows already had it)
The profile's On hold shelf and Up next say **"S3 returns 14 Nov"** for series that are paused or caught up (TMDB's next episode to air is already fetched by continue-watching; one cached read). Gap #11: Apple drops a caught-up show from Up Next and everyone hates it. A paused series with a return date is a reason to keep it on hold rather than drop it.

### 5.3 On this day · S · **already there** (Bet 6: On This Day on Home, positive memories only)
A stub from a year ago on the profile and Home, only positive ones, led by the person who was there ("A year ago, with Priya: Past Lives"). The research's strongest mood-lifting mechanic, and it costs one read of the diary.

### 5.4 Decide, better · S · **already there** (Tonight favours what you've all saved, skips what anyone's seen; Next keeps a title out)
Tonight gets two chips on each candidate — **"seen it"** and **"not tonight"** — and "Pick one for me" favours what both people saved. Deciding together is the mode that swipe apps got wrong and died on; letsee has it, and it should be the fastest thing in the app.

## 6. Unique, longer term

### 6.1 A room for a series · L · **built 13 Oct**
Group rooms exist. A group on the same series gets the episode-gated reactions of 4.1 plus a "who's where" bar (everyone's position, from the series control). The buddy-read, for television. No product does this for a group of friends today.

### 6.2 A supporter tier that sells something people already want · L · **pledges open 25 Oct** (`/app/support`: $20 a year, ₹1,700 in India; no payments yet — migration 127)
Export, privacy and everything free stay free. The tier sells a *thing*: the stub-image pack with no corner mark, a printed year (twelve month cards as postcards), and early access. StoryGraph pre-sold 1,400 of a product that didn't exist; pre-sell this before building it.

### 6.3 Seen at the cinema, by city · M · **built 13 Oct** (by watch region — letsee keeps no city)
Logs already carry "at the cinema". A city shelf — "what people here saw at the cinema this month" — from that field and the account's region, with no listings API and nothing to pay for. The repertory audience is one of the seed tribes the research named.

## 7. Simplify the big things

### 7.1 One sheet for every “more” · S · **decided against for now** (the plan box is visible where it's needed; ⋯ would hide it)
Watch later's plan (why, when, with whom) and the diary's details (when, who, stars, words) are the same shape. Make them the same sheet, reached the same way (⋯). One pattern, learned once, for the whole app.

### 7.2 Home as three questions · M · **built 13 Oct** (what you're watching, each show with its next episode → waiting on you → your people and six of their week; the rest under one line)
"What are you on?" (next episodes, what you're watching), "Who's waiting?" (5.1), "What's new here?" (the first six of Activity). Everything else folds beneath. A page that asks three questions is a page someone understands before they scroll.

### 7.3 Say what a mark does, once · S · **built 12 Oct**
The first time someone taps each mark, a one-line note under the toast: "Watched means seen — add the day from ⋯ when you want to". Shown once per account per mark, then never. The cheapest tutorial is the one that arrives at the moment of the question.

---

## What I'd still not build

Restating the research so these stop coming back: streaks and friendship streaks; leaderboards and ranks of any kind; badges or achievements; a feed that isn't chronological, friends-first and finite; anything the app starts on its own (a daily prompt, "time to log"); a percentage on taste; stranger comment threads without a moderation budget; paywalling export or privacy; short video; hours on the profile header.
